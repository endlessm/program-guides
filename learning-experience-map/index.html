What the tags mean

AI native

The program is built to teach AI itself. Making with AI is the whole point, and the work is judged on how well the learner prompts, evaluates, and refines.

AI touchpoint

AI shows up in part of the work, usually as a workflow helper, not as the point of the program. The learner can lean on it or skip it.

A note on Threadbare and AI. The Threadbare game stays human-made by design: the large majority of contributions are not AI-created, which keeps the world authentic. AI is welcome in support of the workflow, but it does not make the game's assets.

The AI skills tool we are building

We are building a prototype of an interactive tool that behaves like a real AI on one side and teaches on the other. Learners prompt it, judge what comes back, and refine, while it quietly captures every prompt, revision, and decision. It assesses the process of using AI, how well a learner prompts, iterates, and evaluates, not just the output.

It is grounded in Ethan and Lilach Mollick's framework. Their "Assigning AI: Seven Approaches for Students" lays out roles for AI in learning, tutor, coach, tool, and more, with the student kept as the human in the loop who directs and critiques the machine. The tool puts the learner in that seat: AI is the instrument, the learner is the author, and the skill we grade is their judgment over it. That follows Mollick's wider case in Co-Intelligence that the goal is to work with AI well, not to avoid it.

It gives a defensible answer to the question everyone asks: how do we know a learner can actually use AI? We can show their process against a recognized framework, not just a finished artifact.
